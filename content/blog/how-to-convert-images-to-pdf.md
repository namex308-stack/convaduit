Converting images to PDF is useful when a form, email, or document portal accepts one file instead of a collection of photos. Before exporting, decide the page order and whether each image should fill the page or sit on a standard paper size.

## Prepare the images

1. Choose the clearest version of each image. Retake blurry or unreadable pages instead of expecting PDF conversion to restore detail.
2. Put the files in reading order. For a multipage scan, check that the first page comes first.
3. Check for glare, shadows, cut-off edges, and sensitive details that should be redacted from the image itself.
4. Consider file size. High-resolution camera photos can make a large PDF; lower-resolution exports may be easier to send but can make small text harder to read.

## Choose page size and fit

- **Fit to image** keeps a page close to the image’s proportions, which can be useful for receipts and photographs.
- **A4, Letter, or Legal** gives every page a standard paper size for printing or submission.
- **Orientation and margins** affect the available space. Check that no edge or text is cut off and that the image is not stretched.

The best choice depends on where the PDF will be viewed or printed. Check any requirements from the recipient before converting.

## Create one PDF from your images

1. Open the [Image to PDF tool](/tools/image-to-pdf).
2. Add your supported image files and move them into the intended page order.
3. Choose a page size, orientation, margins, and quality setting.
4. Create the PDF, then open it to check page order, readability, and file size.

The tool creates the PDF in your browser. In its lossless setting, supported JPEG and PNG files are embedded directly; other inputs or quality settings may be rasterized through the browser, so the output need not be pixel-identical to every source. Photo pages are images in a PDF, not searchable text. Use OCR separately if you need searchable or selectable words.

If your goal is to turn an existing PDF into image files, use the [PDF to Image tool](/tools/pdf-to-image). For an oversized result, see the guide to [reducing PDF file size](/blog/reduce-pdf-file-size).
